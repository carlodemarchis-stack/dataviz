Method and sources

Every number in this film comes from the text layer of the English PDF of the Gruppo Florence Sustainability Report 2025, 97 pages and 331,000 characters, read page by page. Nothing is interpolated or estimated; where the report gives no figure the card says so. The year buttons at the bottom are the single state: every card re-sorts to the selected year and shows the previous year as a ghost bar where it helps.

The report

GRI Standards 2021 with reference; limited assurance by EY S.p.A. under ISAE 3000 Revised, with some indicators excluded; impact materiality only; CSRD reporting expected from FY2027. Published by Gruppo Florence in June 2026 on the Auxilium page of gruppoflorence.it.

Where each card comes from

  • Emissions, energy, intensity: environment chapter, intensity on p. 37; Scope 2 location-based is given for 2025 only.
  • Water, materials and waste: environment chapter; the 2025 renewable materials share reflects a methodological refinement.
  • Targets: SBTi commitment, SA8000 plan and milestones as published; progress bars and the straight-line path are computed here.
  • Structure: divisions, board and group perimeter from the governance chapter.
  • People: pp. 68 to 81. Grade, age, training, pay, hires and exits are Italy only; foreign companies appear as headcount totals. Injury hours: 2023 estimated, 2024 excludes SNG, Logama and foreign companies, 2025 full scope.
  • Economic value: pp. 57 to 58. 2023 is the sum of statutory accounts net of intercompany items; 2024 and 2025 are from the IFRS consolidated statements, so the bases are not strictly comparable.
  • Economic value by division: Table 20, p. 58, the same two bases as above.
  • Supply chain: Tables 23 to 30, pp. 62 to 64 (processing supplier assessments, spend coverage, acquisitions) and Table 9, p. 40 (material certifications by division).

Known gaps

  • Scope 3 categories 9 to 12 not available for 2023; 8 and 13 to 15 not reported; category 1 limited to materials the group considers relevant.
  • Renewable electricity: the letter says 100% of Italian sites, a footnote says close to full because of contract timing; the energy table is used.
  • No figures by brand, by material or by laboratory; nothing below division level.

The 2025 sustainability report, card by card

Gruppo Florence groups 39 Italian laboratories that make ready-to-wear, footwear, leather goods and intermediate processing for the luxury brands. Its 2025 sustainability report covers three years of a group that grew by acquisition and then slowed with the market: 4,627 people, 588 million euros of economic value generated, Scope 1 and 2 emissions down 43% while Scope 3 rose to 383,000 tonnes, water and materials following the perimeter, SA8000 spreading across the sites, and a cost base that outran revenue for two years running. Each card below takes one chapter of the report and lets you read it year by year.

37 manufacturing divisions, more than 100 brands, nine regions, and a board that went from two women to four

Ready-to-wear is 25 of the 37 divisions. The parent board grew to eleven in 2024. The report states that no formal structure yet ensures direct Board involvement in ESG; a Strategic Council reviews it monthly and a six-member Supply Chain Committee meets monthly.

10 · Economic value by division

In — — of the four divisions retained less than they distributed; ready-to-wear generated — of the group's value

The group's negative retained value is not one division's problem. Ready-to-wear held its revenue at around 470 million across the three years while its distributed value rose 40 million, so it turned negative in 2025. Footwear lost 40% of its value generated in 2024 and has retained a loss of 14 to 17 million since. Intermediate processing shrank by four fifths after 2023 as work moved inside the garment divisions. Leather goods doubled with acquisitions and still retains a loss.

11 · Supply chain

In — the group assessed — processing suppliers and found significant social impacts at — of them

Gruppo Florence sits in the middle of the chain: the brands audit it, and it audits the façonisti who do embroidery, assembly, washing and printing for its laboratories. Coverage is built on spend rather than headcount: 65% of active suppliers but 95% of processing spend were assessed in 2025. The jump in significant social findings, from 43% to 66%, comes from a stricter checklist (a 1 to 5 scale with the option of terminating the relationship), so it reads as tighter scrutiny rather than a worse supply base. Environmental findings stay rare because most processing is low-impact; the checks concentrate on printing, washing, upper-making and dyeing.

Source: Tables 23 to 30, pp. 62 to 64; Table 9, p. 40. "Significant" means a qualification outcome of 1 to 2 on the 2024 scale and 1 to 3 on the 2025 scale. No suppliers came in through acquisitions in 2025.
